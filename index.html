Treatments

Complete dental care under one roof

Choose a category to see related treatments. Tap any treatment to read more.

  • A routine examination lets the dentist review your teeth, gums, bite and the soft tissues of the mouth.

    Finding a cavity or gum concern early often means simpler treatment. How often you need a check-up depends on your oral health, and your dentist will suggest a suitable interval.

  • X-rays help assess areas between teeth, the roots and the supporting bone.

    They are taken only when clinically needed, and the dentist will explain why an image is recommended for you.

  • The decayed part of the tooth is removed and the space is restored with a suitable filling material, often tooth-coloured.

    The material and approach depend on the size and position of the cavity.

  • Scaling removes hardened deposits (tartar) that brushing alone cannot remove. Polishing then smooths the tooth surface.

    Regular professional cleaning supports gum health. Your dentist will advise how often you need it.

  • Whitening can lighten the shade of natural teeth. Results differ from person to person, and whitening does not change the colour of crowns or fillings.

    A check-up first helps confirm whether whitening is suitable for you.

  • When the soft inner part of a tooth (the pulp) is infected or inflamed, root canal treatment cleans, shapes and seals the canals inside the root.

    It is carried out under local anaesthesia. A crown may be advised afterwards to protect the tooth.

  • A crown covers the visible part of a tooth to protect it and restore its shape.

    Crowns are often advised after root canal treatment or for heavily filled or cracked teeth.

  • A bridge fills the gap left by one or more missing teeth using the teeth on either side for support.

    Your dentist will check whether the supporting teeth are healthy enough for a bridge.

  • Bleeding or swollen gums can be an early sign of gum disease.

    Treatment may include deep cleaning, oral hygiene guidance and regular reviews, depending on your condition.

  • An implant is a small titanium post placed in the jaw bone to support a crown, bridge or denture.

    Suitability depends on bone support, gum health and general health, which are evaluated before any plan is made.

  • Dentures replace missing teeth to help with chewing, speech and appearance.

    A complete denture replaces all teeth in a jaw; a partial denture fills gaps between remaining teeth.

  • Braces apply gentle, controlled pressure to move teeth over time.

    They are used for a wide range of alignment and bite concerns. Regular adjustment visits are part of treatment.

  • Clear aligners are a series of custom trays, each moving the teeth a small step.

    They need to be worn for most of the day as advised. Suitability and duration are decided after a clinical assessment.

  • Veneers are thin coverings bonded to the front surface of teeth.

    They may help with chipped, discoloured or uneven teeth. Some tooth preparation is usually needed, which your dentist will explain.

  • Smile designing looks at tooth shape, shade, alignment and gums together.

    The plan may combine several treatments and is discussed with you before anything begins.

  • Wisdom teeth sometimes lack space to come through properly and may cause pain or swelling.

    An examination and X-ray help decide whether monitoring or removal is advised.

  • Extraction is considered when a tooth is badly damaged, loose or causing problems.

    It is done under local anaesthesia, and aftercare instructions are given to support healing.

  • Early visits help children feel at ease with the dentist and allow problems to be spotted early.

    Care may include check-ups, cleaning, fluoride application and guidance for parents.

  • The TMJ is the joint that connects your lower jaw to your skull.

    If you have jaw pain, clicking or headaches around the jaw, an assessment helps understand possible causes and management options.

  • Full mouth rehabilitation may be considered when many teeth are worn, damaged or missing.

    It combines different treatments in a planned sequence, discussed step by step with you.

  • Prevention includes regular examinations, professional cleaning and advice on brushing, flossing and diet.

    Sealants or fluoride may be suggested where appropriate.

  • Restorative dentistry covers fillings, crowns, bridges, dentures and implant-supported teeth.

    The right option depends on the tooth’s condition and your needs.

  • If you have severe tooth pain, swelling, a broken tooth or a knocked-out tooth, call the clinic as soon as possible.

    If swelling affects breathing or swallowing, go to the nearest hospital emergency department immediately.

Treatment recommendations are made only after a clinical examination. Suitability, number of visits and outcomes vary from person to person.

Orthodontic options

Clear aligners or braces?

Both can straighten teeth. They simply work differently in daily life. Here is a fair side-by-side view.

Clear aligners
Traditional braces
Appearance
Nearly transparent trays that are hard to notice.
Brackets and wires are visible; tooth-coloured options may be available.
Removability
Removable, but must be worn for most of the day as advised.
Fixed to the teeth throughout treatment.
Cleaning routine
Remove to brush and floss normally; aligners need regular cleaning.
Brushing around brackets takes extra care and sometimes special brushes.
Food restrictions
Remove aligners before eating or drinking anything other than water.
Hard and sticky foods are best avoided.
Treatment suitability
Suitable for many alignment concerns; decided after assessment.
Suitable for a wide range of cases, including many complex ones.
Follow-up
Periodic reviews and new aligner sets as planned.
Periodic visits for adjustments.

Digital dentistry

See your smile with digital precision

A digital intraoral scan captures a detailed 3D image of your teeth using a small handheld wand—often in place of traditional impression material.

  • Comfortable digital impressions

    No bulky trays of impression paste for most scans.

  • Detailed 3D visualisation

    See your teeth on screen from every angle.

  • Support for aligner planning

    Scans help plan tooth movement for clear aligners.

  • Clearer consultations

    Visuals make it easier to understand your options.

  • Digital treatment records

    Records that support planning and follow-up.

Digital scanning is used where clinically available and appropriate for your treatment.
